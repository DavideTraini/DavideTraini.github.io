Research

Davide Traini's research mainly concerns Artificial Intelligence, with a particular focus on the development of efficient, interpretable and reliable models. His work centers on the study of Vision Transformers, Large Language Models, Knowledge Graphs and Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) methodologies.

Research topics

Efficient Vision Transformers

Development of token pruning strategies and selection of the most informative components, based on attention mechanisms or Reinforcement Learning, in order to reduce model complexity while preserving performance.

The optimization of Vision Transformers is one of Davide Traini's main research lines, motivated by the need to reduce the computational cost of these models without compromising their performance. [1] proposes a first approach based on Reinforcement Learning to speed up ViTs, dynamically identifying the most informative patches to process and discarding the redundant ones. This work was later extended in [2], where adaptive patch selection is integrated into a more mature framework, able to adapt to different architectures and to ensure a better trade-off between accuracy and inference speed. In [3] the token pruning problem is addressed from a complementary perspective, by introducing a multilayer network based on attention weights to identify the tokens that are least relevant for classification, showing how network theory can provide effective tools also for the structural optimization of models. The topic of token reduction is also addressed with signal-processing tools in [4], where a technique based on the discrete wavelet decomposition is proposed to identify and remove the least informative tokens, thus offering an alternative and complementary perspective to those based on attention or Reinforcement Learning.

Explainable Artificial Intelligence

Development of novel frameworks to analyze the behavior of Vision Transformers and to make the relationships among tokens and the models' decision-making processes more understandable.

In parallel with the work on efficiency, Davide Traini has investigated the explainability of Vision Transformers, adopting tools from Network Science. [5] proposes a multiplex representation of ViTs, in which the relationships among tokens across the different layers and attention heads are modeled as a multilayer network, making it possible to identify recurring patterns in the way the model processes visual information. This line is further developed in [6], where attention is mapped to a similarity graph among tokens, which is exploited to generate visual explanations of the model's decisions. In [7] this approach is enriched by integrating gradient-based techniques with mask-based methods, in order to obtain explanations that are more robust and complementary to those obtainable with a single method, confirming the usefulness of combining different perspectives in explainability analysis. The line based on similarity graphs finds a further development in [8], where such graphs are used to directly generate relevance masks, making the production of explanations more direct and more tightly integrated with the structural representation of the model.

Knowledge Graphs and Large Language Models

Study of the integration of graph-based semantic representations and LLMs, to make model training more efficient and to improve performance in RAG systems.

A further research area concerns the integration of graph-based semantic representations and modern Large Language Models. [9] proposes a method that exploits the communities detected within a Knowledge Graph to guide the fine-tuning of an LLM, improving the semantic coherence of the generated answers. This approach is taken up and refined in [10], which introduces a strategy based on ego networks to selectively choose the most relevant portions of knowledge to use in the training phase, with the goal of making the process more efficient even in the presence of large graphs. With a view to further enhancing Retrieval-Augmented Generation systems, [11] proposes a framework that integrates cross-attention mechanisms and contrastive learning with graph representations, improving the system's ability to retrieve and combine relevant information while generating answers.

Artificial Intelligence in Education

Use of Artificial Intelligence to support the learning of students with special educational needs, or as a tool to assist teachers.

Alongside the more methodological topics, Davide Traini has also worked on applications of Artificial Intelligence in education, with particular attention to inclusion. [12] analyzes the behavioral engagement of students learning sign language in virtual reality environments, using visual attention as a predictor of performance and of the temporal dynamics of learning. This work is part of a broader line of research aimed at understanding how immersive technologies and Artificial Intelligence can support students with special educational needs and give teachers more effective tools to personalize learning paths. The work described in [13] is instead set in a more strictly didactic context, addressing the improper use of Large Language Models by students during assessments. The goal is the automatic generation of multiple-choice quizzes built with adversarial attack techniques, designed to be hard for an LLM to answer correctly while remaining clear and approachable for a student, so as to discourage the use of these tools to answer assessments improperly.

Other research topics

Research on topics beyond the main lines, concerning social network analysis and the use of Large Language Models in application contexts other than those described above.

Alongside the main research lines, Davide Traini has carried out studies on further topics that cannot be directly traced back to the previous areas. [14] analyzes the dynamics of user influence on the Threads platform, investigating how specific interaction patterns contribute to the spread of information within the network. [15] instead explores the ability of Large Language Models to act as scientific reviewers, comparing their performance with that of human reviewers on OpenReview, an investigation that fits into the broader debate on the reliability and limits of generative AI in tasks that require critical and expert judgment. A further contribution in an applied domain is [16], which proposes a hybrid framework combining a FinBERT model with a Graph Attention Network (GAT) to predict a company's ESG (Environmental, Social and Governance) score.
